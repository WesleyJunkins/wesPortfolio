## 6. What Dr. Olson's advice means for this content (context for Claude Code; from Wes's Oct 8, 2026 call with Dr. Danielle Olson, Apple)

These are the reasons behind the choices above:
- **"Decks are currency. Simplify."** Apple (and industry research generally) judges communication through artifacts. Every project blurb should have one takeaway, not a paragraph of mechanism.
- **Human Engineering / human-factors teams do study design and execution** — protocols, moderating, instruments. The site should make Wes's study-execution evidence unmistakable: IRB protocols written, n = 13 within-subjects study run, 27-student evaluation, NASA-TLX/SUS/IMI. That's why the BDR-XR and PhysioBots entries lead with methods and participants.
- **Evaluation is "the hardest HCI problem" in industry AI right now.** The spine's third layer, and the framing of the voting project around trust and evaluation, speak directly to that.
- **Multimodal signal → meaning is the direction every company cares about.** Keep "physiological computing" broad (wearables, biometrics), not just EEG.
- **Applied beats theoretical for internships.** Status tags, released software (PSL), and shipped prototypes (voting hardware) are deliberate.
- **Case-study pages** (one page per featured project, with a problem → approach → result → takeaway structure) are the next content step after this update, and will become the interview portfolio. This brief gets the facts and framing right first; case studies can be added as new pages later without changing the design.
